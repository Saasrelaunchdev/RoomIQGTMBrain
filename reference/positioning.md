# Positioning

## Category
Room-level market intelligence and underwriting, for US rent-by-the-room housing.

## Positioning statement
For US rent-by-the-room investors deciding where to buy, what a house will earn, and how to price its rooms, RoomIQ is room-level market intelligence and underwriting that shows its evidence. Short-term rental analytics model nightly stays and rent comps model whole units; RoomIQ works in the unit this business runs on, the room, priced weekly. And where a spreadsheet hides its guesses, every RoomIQ forecast prints its formula, its comps, and how much to trust it.

## The three things RoomIQ leads on
1. **The room is the unit.** Weekly room rents, room occupancy, fill speed, and bath and size segments (C14, C15, C12).
2. **Evidence you can see.** Formula printed, support labeled, comp distance disclosed, confidence labeled (C27, C28, C16).
3. **Rooms that look alike.** Visual comps with weekly price and days to fill (C17).

## Reframes, in order of use
- From "What will this house rent for?" to "What will each room earn, and how sure can I be?"
- From "a confident number" to "a number that tells you how far to trust it".
- From "comps nearby" to "rooms that look like yours, wherever they are".

## What positioning must not drift into
- An accuracy story (D-05).
- A data-scale story (no coverage numbers, G13).
- An "AI underwriting" story. AI framing belongs only to visual comps and AI concepts (G19).
- A short-term rental story. Different unit, different buyer.
- A marketplace story. RoomIQ lists nothing and books nothing.

## Defensibility, internal only
Room-level history compounds with time: every observed day deepens the evidence behind scores and forecasts. The transparency posture is hard to retrofit onto tools built around one confident number. Neither point is a public claim until coverage and history depth are confirmed (G13).
