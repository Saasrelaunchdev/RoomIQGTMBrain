# Example, community answer for operators

Status: draft gold standard, checked against ledgers 2026-11-13, founder approval pending.
Audience: B, operating hosts. Template: `templates/community-answer.md`.
The question, as asked: "Two rooms in my house keep sitting empty for weeks while the others fill fast. Same price. What am I missing?"

---

Same price is probably the problem. Rooms in one house rarely earn the same, and residents compare them the way you would.

Start with the bathroom. A room with a private bath competes in a different market from a room that shares one. If three rooms share a single bath, those three are fighting each other and every private-bath room nearby. Look at the rooms that fill fast: do they have the bath, the bigger footprint, the window?

Then check fill time, not just occupancy. A room that sits 30 days and then rents at full price may be costing you more than one that rents in 3 days at $15 less a week. Over a quarter, the empty weeks usually win that argument.

What I'd do: list each room with its bath type, size, and how many days it sat last time. Price the slow ones against similar rooms nearby, not against the rest of your house.

Disclosure: I'm building RoomIQ, which breaks properties into room segments by bath and size and shows rooms that look like yours with their weekly price and days to fill. Happy to look at your two rooms with you if useful.
